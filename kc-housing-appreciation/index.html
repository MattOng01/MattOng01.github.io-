Kansas City Home Price Appreciation (2000–2026)

Matthew Ong · August 2026

RStudio QGIS Leaflet Tableau

Cheaper ZIP codes in the Kansas City metro are catching up to expensive ones — and the pattern holds even after accounting for the fact that home prices cluster geographically, not randomly.

Formally: this project tests for β-convergence, σ-convergence, and spatial dependence across 198 KC-metro ZIP codes using Zillow Home Value Index (ZHVI) data, 2000–2026.

Practical takeaway for analysts

Because appreciation clusters geographically, comps are more reliable when pulled by radius around a subject property than restricted to a single ZIP code — a ZIP-bounded comp set will miss real spillover effects from adjacent areas.

Why It Matters

→

For investors and analysts: identifies ZIP codes experiencing rapid appreciation and clarifies the right comp radius to use, since appreciation spills over between adjacent areas rather than stopping at ZIP boundaries.

→

For planners and policymakers: shows that convergence is real, not just a spillover artifact — and that rising housing wealth in historically lower-priced neighborhoods raises real questions about affordability and displacement alongside the opportunity.

Methods

To evaluate home price appreciation, I identified key economic behavior and tested for and addressed spatial autocorrelation. The full derivations, regression tables, and diagnostics are in the full report; the summary below is meant to be readable without a stats background.

Beta convergence → Are lower-priced areas catching up?
Sigma convergence → Is price dispersion shrinking?
Spatial autocorrelation → Are nearby neighborhoods behaving similarly?
Spatial Error Model → Accounts for the influence of geographic clustering to isolate the underlying relationship.

Future Research
