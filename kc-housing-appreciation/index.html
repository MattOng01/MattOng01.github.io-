Kansas City Home Price Appreciation (2000–2026)

Matthew Ong · August 2026

RStudio QGIS Leaflet Tableau

This project examines whether lower-priced neighborhoods in the Kansas City metropolitan area are catching up to higher-priced neighborhoods. Using Zillow Home Value Index (ZHVI) data for 198 ZIP codes, I test for β-convergence, σ-convergence, and spatial dependence in home price appreciation.

Understanding these patterns is valuable for investors, developers, planners, and policymakers seeking to understand neighborhood change and housing market dynamics.

Lower-priced ZIP codes appreciated faster than higher-priced ZIP codes.

Why It Matters

Identifies areas experiencing rapid appreciation.

Helps planners understand changing neighborhood dynamics.

Demonstrates that appreciation is occurring beyond simple geographic spillovers.

Suggests that rising housing wealth in historically lower-priced neighborhoods could create opportunities—but also raises questions about affordability and displacement.

Methods

To evaluate home price apperciation, I identified key economic behavior and tested for and addressed spatial autocorrelation.

Beta convergence → Are lower-priced areas catching up?
Sigma convergence → Is price dispersion shrinking?
Spatial autocorrelation → Are nearby neighborhoods behaving similarly?
Spatial Error Model → Accounts for the influence of geographic clustering to isolate the underlying relationship.

Future Research

This analysis uses home prices as a proxy for neighborhood change. Future work could examine whether appreciation is accompanied by improvements in:

  • school quality
  • crime
  • incomes
  • neighborhood amenities
  • transit accessibility
  • housing supply
  • displacement and affordability
