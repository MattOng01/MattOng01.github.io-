Overview

This project looks at two growth economics phenomena: beta and sigma convergence.

One theory of economic growth, formalized by Robert Solow in 1956 illistrates how economices grow at different rates depending on their level of economic output. In the long run, Solow argued, economies converge at a stable level of output, so more affluent economies would grow relatavely slower as they approach this level compared to less affluent economies.

This has important implications for real estate, urban planning, and policy.

This growth convergence has two related but different identifiers, called beta and sigma convergence.

Beta convergence occurs when less affluent economies grow faster than more affluent economies.

Sigma convergence occurs when the gap between rich and poor economies shrinks (the standard deviation of their log growth becomes smaller across time).

In Kansas City, there is evidence of both Beta and Sigma convergence in ZIP-code level house prices from 2000-2026.
